# Fix batch 3 — 2026-09-11

Owner reports from the pre-endgame build, and what changed for each. Everything below is in the
working tree that produced `release/sliced-2.0.0-fix-batch-3.zip`.

## Live-progress chips with no text

The telemetry and debugger pills rendered icon-only. The text *was* in the DOM at `opacity: 0`:
`pill.update` fades the label in with `fill: "both"`, and the live view's first render happens
while the view is still detached (the store replays its snapshot synchronously on subscribe, before
`container.append(root)`). A detached node has no timeline, so the animation stayed play-pending at
its first keyframe forever. The hand pill escaped because its text keeps changing. Fix:
`src/panel/components/pill.ts` fades only when `text.isConnected`.

## The board mark changing after it was shown

There is no shallow-then-deep search. The same position was being **re-run**: chess.com republishes
the unmoved position when the exact FEN, the colour or the time control arrives (the time control
lands on such a republish at move 1 in nearly every live game), and move selection sampled a shared
RNG, so the second run landed on a different move; `clearBoardMarks()` before every run also reset
the overlay's dedupe, so even an unchanged move replayed its fade-in. Three changes
(`src/service/game-session/session.ts`):

- `holdForTimeControl`: the game's first position waits `TIMINGS.timeControlGraceMs` (1.5 s) for the
  site's time control before anything is searched, drawn or scheduled; the republish releases it,
  the timer releases a page that never answers (`/play/computer`).
- Selection is seeded per position (`gameSeed:ply:board`), so identical inputs give the identical
  move and only a genuinely changed input (the lines, the budget) changes it. No first-come bias —
  each position's draw is as random as before, just reproducible within that position.
- A same-board re-run no longer clears the mark; a changed move still replaces it.

`test/behavioral/game/first-position-hold.test.ts` pins all three.

## Active Elo after a mid-game reload

The content script reads the opponent card once, at `document_start`, before chess.com has rendered
it, and never again; the port replay on a worker restart did not include `opponent` either. A
rating-less opponent is what `targetElo()` treats as "no opponent", i.e. the slider's Elo. Fix: the
read retries every `TIMINGS.opponentReadRetryMs` up to `opponentReadRetryMax` until a rating is
found (`src/content/index.ts`), and `feed-port.ts` replays the last `opponent` with `hello`.

## Premoves too rare

`PREMOVE` (`src/core/constants/books.ts`): reflex probability 0.55 → 0.95 across the Elo span (was
0.35 → 0.85), reply confidence 0.55 (was 0.6; still refuses a coin flip between near-equals),
clear-best gap 0.18 (was 0.25), site-queue entry window 150–700 ms (was 350–1200 ms, measured after
~340 ms of arming searches, so the opponent usually replied first), prediction search 220 ms (was
150). The per-tab "site drops premoves" latch is unchanged: it trips only on a completed gesture
that provably did not land.

## More "thinking" piece selections

`DEFAULT_SETTINGS.execution.previewSelectScale` 1 → 1.1. The ceiling is the telemetry contract's
human population band (`TELEMETRY_BANDS.multiSelect.rate`, 4–12 % of eligible moves): defaults sat
at ≈8.7 %; 1.3× measured ≈11 % on its own, but once the quick-reply regime (below) thinned the
eligible pool it read 13.5 % in the 12-game conformance batch, so 1.1× (≈11.4 %) is where it can
sit. Anything higher breaches the band; the band and conformance tests were re-run.

## The hold

During the opponent's turn the hand may pick up the piece it expects to move (the armed §7.4
premove, else the ponder's top line's answer), carry it to the destination and hold it there
(`HandState` `holding`); their move is the cue to let go — after a sampled human reaction
(`SCRAMBLE_HOLD.releaseReactionMs`, 110–240 ms) — so the move lands the way a human hovering the
piece drops it. How often (`SCRAMBLE_HOLD`): 8 % of opponent turns in ordinary play, rising
linearly from 10 s left on our clock to 85 % at 7 s and below; and from 10 s left on the
*opponent's* clock to 55 % at 5 s (a time-pressed opponent bangs moves out, and a hovering hand is
how a human keeps up) — the higher of the two, never certain. The decision is not made at the
position but at checkpoints during their turn (`decisionFirstMs` in, then every
`decisionIntervalMs`, at most four), each rolling a front-loaded share of the turn's odds, so a long
think can turn into a hold — or a premove entry, spread the same way — midway through the
exploration. The position that
arrives is checked first: the held move must still be legal and must not simply hang the piece
(`src/core/chess/safety.ts`); in ordinary play the hand then keeps it only 60 % of the time when
the reply was the predicted one and 30 % otherwise — "hold moves are generally weaker", so seeing
the reply often means taking the ready move back for a searched one (a scramble has no such
second thoughts). Otherwise, on a timeout drawn per hold (8–15 s in a scramble, 2.5–7 s in
ordinary play — never a fixed N) or on any cancel (disarm, disable, game end), the hand carries the
piece **back to its origin square** and lets go there, never on the destination. The candidate
itself is a notch weaker than a searched move: the armed §7.4 premove when there is one, else the
ordinary selector run over the predicted position's pre-analysis at 250 Elo below the target, else
— half the time — the ponder's own answer. The reaction between seeing their move and letting go
is right-skewed over 110–650 ms (`min + range·u²`): most near the floor, a real tail. Reported hold
time is measured from the release decision, matching what the page measures.
`test/behavioral/game/scramble-hold.test.ts` asserts release (or a truthful take-back), timeout and
disarm on dispatched input.

## After the drop: rest or ponder, decided at once

The hand no longer sits on the piece it just moved until the opponent replies. After the drop it
pauses `EXECUTOR.postDropLingerMs` (40–160 ms) and decides: `postDropRestProb` (55 %) of the time
it walks — a generated path, gated like the rest of the move — to a random piece, ours or theirs,
never the one just moved, weighted toward the centre of the board (`postDropCentreBias`), and rests
there `postDropRestMs` (250–800 ms); otherwise the execution ends and the opponent-turn exploration
("pondering") begins at once. The exploration's own settle before its first bout is shortened to
`OPPONENT_EXPLORATION.initialRestMs` 400–900 ms (was 1–2.2 s) since the resting is decided here.
Fast-touch moves (premoves, races) skip it as before.

## Input mode

`Settings.execution.inputMode` — `auto` (default), `drag`, `click`. This reverses the owner's
2026-09-10 drag-only ruling at the owner's request (2026-09-11), as a setting so it is one toggle
either way. `click` commits a move as click-click: click the piece, carry the pointer over with the
button up (`CLICK_MOVE.interClickGapMs` between), click the square; `auto` draws a click per move
from its own RNG stream (so the motor's per-move sampling is unchanged by the choice) with a share
that depends on how far the piece travels (`src/core/motor/input-style.ts`, owner's follow-up):
`CLICK_MOVE.autoClickProb` (34 %) for a hop under `farSquares` (4) squares, only `autoClickProbFar`
(12 %) for a carry of four or more — a human clicks to nudge and drags to carry. Premoves, holds
and promotions are always drags. A click-click
interrupted between its clicks (veto, abort, reflow) leaves a piece selected on the site, which
§13.7 item 3 forbids: the hand first clicks an idle square (empty, not a legal destination — the
preview module's own deselect) in the geometry the page has now, then unwinds. The timing model
still plans every window as a drag (`test/behavioral/game/input-method.test.ts` guards that); the
click leg is fitted to the same touch plan. `ExecutionResult.tier` names the gesture, and the
game-test harness defaults to `drag` so press-counting tests keep their meaning
(`test/behavioral/game/input-mode.test.ts` covers click, drag and auto).

## Sliders snapping back while the hand moves

The settings view re-applies the *stored* value to every slider on every store snapshot, and the
slider took it even mid-drag — so with snapshots arriving on every hand pointer event, the thumb was
yanked back to the old value between pointer moves. The slider now ignores external values while
the pointer owns it, and for `UI_TIMINGS.sliderCommitGraceMs` after a commit ignores values that
disagree with the one it just wrote (the store's next snapshot can still carry the pre-write
value). `test/panel/components/slider.test.ts` pins both.

## The obvious reply

Owner: "waits way too long on recaptures … where there's only one way to retake". The shipped
ChessMimic head has no regime for that (the `forced`/`recap` multipliers belong to the parametric
fallback), so a forced move was paced like a decision. `TIMING_CONSTANTS.quickReply`: after the
head, a move that is forced (`is_forced`) or the one reasonable recapture (`is_recapture` with
`n_reasonable == 1`) is capped to `U(0.6, 1.7) s`, never below the physical gesture, and a `long`
plan becomes `normal`. A genuine choice between two close recaptures is untouched.
`test/core/timing/quick-reply.test.ts` pins both.

## Floors on the rushed move

Owner: "still making superhuman speed movements" at the very end. Two floors, both raised:

- `FAST_TOUCH.gestureFloorMs` (new, 150 ms): approach + press + carry + drop never total less,
  whatever the plan's window says; `minBudgetMs` 20 → 60 for a plan fitted after its deadline.
- `TIMING_CONSTANTS.clockRace`: own-clock windows `moveMinMs` [160, 60] → [240, 150],
  `moveMaxMs` [420, 130] → [305, 215], `minimumWindowMs` 20 → 60 — kept under the export's 250 ms
  normal-move floor at every own urgency so a race move stays distinguishable in the telemetry.

The opponent-only rush (their clock short, ours fine) keeps the previous batch's varied 300–850 ms
reply window.
